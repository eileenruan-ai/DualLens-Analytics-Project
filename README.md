# DualLens Analytics Project

## About This Project

This project represents an important step in my learning journey at the intersection of **artificial intelligence, data, finance, and supply chain**.

As I continue developing my technical skills, I am especially interested in understanding how AI can support better business decisions. I do not just want to learn how to make an AI system generate an answer. I want to understand how we can evaluate that answer, determine whether it is grounded in reliable information, recognize uncertainty, and know when human judgment is still needed.

Through the DualLens Analytics project, I explored a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) workflow designed for financial research.

The project encouraged me to think about an important question:

**How can we make AI-generated financial insights more reliable, transparent, and useful for real-world decision-making?**

This was a guided learning project, but what became most meaningful to me was understanding the reasoning behind the system and seeing how different parts of an AI workflow work together.

---

## Why Finance and Supply Chain Matter to Me

One of the strongest connections I made during this project was between **financial understanding and supply chain decision-making**.

Supply chain decisions are not only operational decisions. They are also financial decisions.

Choices involving sourcing, inventory, transportation, supplier relationships, production, and risk management all have financial consequences.

Understanding areas such as cost, margins, working capital, cash flow, and supplier financial health can help supply chain professionals make stronger and more informed decisions.

For example, choosing a supplier cannot always be based only on the lowest price. A supplier's financial stability, ability to handle disruption, operating performance, and long-term reliability can also affect the health of the entire supply chain.

That is why I believe financial understanding is an important skill for supply chain professionals.

I am especially interested in how **AI and data analytics can connect financial and operational information** to help businesses identify risks, understand trade-offs, and make better decisions.

At the same time, decisions in finance and supply chain can have significant consequences. That makes the accuracy and reliability of AI-generated insights especially important.

This project helped me better understand why responsible AI should include not only generation, but also **evaluation, confidence, transparency, and human review**.

## What I Learned

One of my biggest takeaways from this project is that getting an AI system to produce an answer is only the beginning.

The next questions are just as important:

**Is the answer supported by the source material?**

**Is the answer accurate?**

**How should the response be evaluated?**

**How confident should we be in the result?**

**When should a human review the answer instead of relying completely on the model?**

Working through these questions helped me understand that building useful AI systems requires more than prompt writing.

Retrieval quality, evaluation criteria, prompt design, confidence thresholds, and human judgment can all influence the reliability of the final output.

I also learned that AI development is an iterative process:

**Build → Evaluate → Learn → Improve**

That mindset was one of the most valuable parts of this project for me.

## My Progress

What I enjoyed most about this project was seeing my thinking change as I worked through it.

At the beginning, I was naturally focused on whether the code worked and whether the model could generate an answer.

As I progressed, I started asking deeper questions:

That shift from simply **building something that works** to **evaluating why it works and how it can improve** is an area I want to continue developing.

This project strengthened my interest in AI systems that are not only powerful, but also **practical, explainable, and trustworthy**.
